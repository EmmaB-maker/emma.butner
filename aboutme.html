$ research-journey
My research started with a simple question: how do we construct reality?

As an undergrad at the University of Washington, I studied how environment and social dynamics shape perception. That's where I fell in love with data manipulation.

Then I discovered active sensing: the idea that perception requires movement. That flipped everything. It pulled me from static lab tasks into VR headsets and motion capture, asking how the body shapes the mind.

Today, as a PhD student in the VAAST Lab at the University of Utah, I use virtual reality to study what happens when your eyes and your body disagree. If you're walking but the world moves faster or slower than expected, how does your brain recalibrate space and time?

These aren't just academic questions. Time warps when you're engaged. Space compresses when you're immersed. Understanding these mechanisms is the key to building better VR, better interfaces, and better experiences.

The VAAST Lab at the University of Utah — studying perception and navigation in VR/AR environments.
